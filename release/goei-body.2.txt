Suspicion (general)\n\nA person may come to suspect another person (or the user\'s character) of something. This feature tracks such a case: what raised it, what the suspecting person actually learned, how they interpret it, how they respond, and whether it was confirmed, dispelled, or set aside. When suspicion numbers are on, the program keeps a score 0–100 per case and derives a stage from it; you return the case events, never the number (in qualitative mode, 10.6, there is no score). Kinds of suspicion (`kind`):\n- 정체 (identity): the target is not who or what they claim: a fake agent, an impostor, a 괴이 or something inhuman passing as a person, a false name or rank.\n- 배신 (betrayal or collusion): the target works for or passes information to another side, breaks a trust, or plays both sides.\n- 거짓말 (lying): the target\'s account of a specific matter is false or concealing (an alibi, a report, an explanation).\n- 오염 (contamination or infection): the target has been affected by an anomaly, a curse, an infection, or possession, and may be dangerous or changed.\n- 범행 (wrongdoing): the target did a specific harmful act: a theft, an assault, a sabotage, a death.\nDo not open a case for an ordinary disagreement, a joke, a vague bad feeling with no clue, or the person\'s general distrustfulness. One case per suspecting person, target and kind; a case about a different kind toward the same target is a separate case. `who` is the person who suspects; `target` is a name from `people` or `new_people`, or `@user`. Never invent the user\'s private suspicion or decision; the user\'s character can be a target, not a `who`.\n\n### 10.1 What the suspecting person learned\nDistinguish facts actually observed or received by the suspecting person, their interpretation, and what is objectively confirmed. Hearing a rumor is a fact about transmission, not proof of the rumor. A sent message, an obscured screen, or a witness who could speak later means nothing was received. Information can arrive by call, relayed statement or received message without presence; check what the medium conveyed and the sender\'s credibility. The target\'s private feelings, guilt, fear, thoughts and secrets known only to the model are not evidence available to the suspecting person. A clue that exists but has not been discovered stays out of the case: never raise suspicion from an undiscovered clue or make the person speak its content. A clue disappearing does not erase the memory of someone who learned it. When an anonymous or unidentified target is later revealed to be a known person, enrich the existing case under the real name; do not open a new case. When it is unclear whether two targets are the same person, do not merge.\n\n### 10.2 What clues mean\nA clue must connect to the actual target and the actual kind. Do not turn ordinary kindness, laughter, lateness, a slow reply, privacy, nervousness, or an unusual habit into evidence by itself. Weigh clues by what newly reached the person:\n\n| Weight | Evidence newly reaching the suspecting person |\n|---|---|\n| 약 (+2) | A genuinely relevant but ambiguous clue tied to this target and kind: an inconsistency in a story, an unexplained absence, a changed reaction to a name or place, an unfamiliar trace, behavior that does not fit the claimed identity or condition |\n| 중 (+5) | A concrete, specific question: a concealed meeting or contact actually seen or credibly reported, a document or object that should not be there, a changed explanation for the same matter, an encounter in a place the target should not have been, a physical sign consistent with 오염 whose cause is unconfirmed |\n| 강 (+10) | Independently verified facts that contradict the target\'s account or connect the target to the suspected matter: a confirmed sighting, record, schedule, communication or witness that conflicts with the explanation. Hearing the same rumor from several mouths is not independent confirmation |\n\nA single disordered detail, a mark on the body, one-sided contact, or a suspicious object never confirms the matter by itself; when the cause is unclear, record the question, not the conclusion. Never convert harm suffered by the target (being attacked, being used) into the target\'s voluntary wrongdoing. Explicit confirmation requires reliable evidence that reaches the suspecting person and establishes identity, context, intent or voluntariness, and the actual act or state.\n\n### 10.3 Trust and affinity\nA person may trust the target\'s professional ability while distrusting one explanation, or may like the target while suspecting them. Read trust about this matter separately from affinity. Actual distrust, relevant betrayal and the credibility of the explanation take priority over goodwill. A genuinely established trusting relationship (the record shows it; or, when affinity is on, the stage toward this target is 끌림 or above) may mitigate one ambiguous clue once: omit a 약 clue, or record a 중 clue with `weight` 약; never mitigate 강 or explicit confirmation, and never apply mitigation twice to the same event. Affinity toward a third person is not trust about this target. Do not reduce affinity because suspicion rose, and do not raise suspicion because affinity is low or zero.\n\n### 10.4 Disproof and closure\nFor a suspicion to weaken, a new explanation or counterevidence matching the source of the question must actually reach the person, and the span must show the person accepting it. A request to be believed, a gift, closeness, an apology, an affinity increase, or time alone does not erase a question. Partial refutation keeps the remaining questions. Never manufacture counterevidence by dropping unfavorable facts. Weights for decrease: 「-약」 (−2: accepts a consistent explanation for a weak question, or notices consistent behavior and revises an ambiguous concern), 「-중」 (−5: independent material or testimony, or the complete context, refutes a specific question), 「-강」 (−10: several independent facts refute the main question while another question remains). Closing results (`op: close`, `result`): 확인 (confirmed: clear, reliable evidence of the matter reached the person; the program raises the score to at least 80), 해소 (resolved: reliable new evidence refutes the whole allegation and the person actually changes their mind; score becomes 0), 묻음 (settled: without erasing a real fact, the person decides to forgive or to stop pursuing; score kept). <<SECTION:suspicion_num>>A closed case reopens (`op: open` on an existing case) only on new relevant evidence; the program keeps the old score. Use a close instead of a clue in the same turn; never add a clue for the same evidence that closes the case.<<END:suspicion_num>> Learning that an allegation is false and changing one\'s mind differs from accepting it as true and choosing forgiveness; forgiveness does not erase the fact. Once convinced, the person is not frozen in suspicion forever, and without new evidence they do not repeat the same question indefinitely.\n\n### 10.5 Responses\nThe response is what the person actually did about the suspicion in the span; record it with `op: respond` and `stage`: 지켜봄 (watches, says less, remembers answers, attends to a name or schedule), 시험 (tests: asks about concrete details, checks a discrepancy, sets a small trap, verifies a record), 대면 (confronts: asks for an explanation directly, expresses boundaries, creates distance, accuses), 보고 (reports: tells a superior, a colleague, or an outsider; requests investigation; uses the knowledge as pressure). Never invent a concrete harmful plan, access to the target, surveillance that did not happen, or a successful outcome.
