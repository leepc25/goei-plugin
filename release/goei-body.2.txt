Perception: who knows what\n\nSeparate the world information the model knows from the information a person actually perceived or learned. A fact in the record, in the status window, in the narration, in another person\'s memory, or in the model\'s context is not automatically known by every person. Check, in order: the nature of the fact, whether a path existed, the observer\'s condition, and the certainty of the interpretation. Never choose a conclusion first and invent a clue or a transmission scene to justify it.\n\n- States of knowing, for `knowledge` entries and for deciding whether a fact may be recorded for a person at all: **observed** (saw, heard, touched it directly), **reported** (was told, read a message, heard a credible report; knows that it was said, not that it is true), **inferred** (concluded from clues the person actually perceived; write as the person\'s interpretation, which can be wrong), **unaware** (no evidence or path; never record, never let the person ask as if they knew), **false belief** (what the person wrongly believes and why; this is not knowledge of the truth).\n- Require an actual path: sight, hearing, touch, a received message, a credible report, prior disclosure, or a supported inference. A sent message, an unanswered call, an obscured screen, or a witness who could speak later is not received information. A real phone, radio or video connection is a path only for what that medium carries: a voice call does not transmit expressions, clothing or touch.\n- A person must be in the path: present to perceive it, explicitly addressed, or explicitly receiving the message, report, disclosure or correction. A scene fact about another person is not knowledge for an absent owner, however true it is. A newcomer does not inherit the knowledge of those already present. Event information does not propagate to another space by itself.\n- More clues may raise confidence without revealing the exact cause, target, place, order, winner or motive. Never read a whole specific secret from anxious body language. Being told that someone is suspicious is not knowledge of the underlying claim.\n- A misunderstanding persists until a correction actually arrives in the span. Never transmit the truth the reader knows into a person\'s mind; never use an unintroduced name, occupation or title for a person who has not learned it.\n- Secrets: track only narratively meaningful ones. A secret this person holds (their own, or one entrusted to them) is one `perception.secret` entry: what it is, who knows it, and who must not; upsert when the circle of knowers changes. The program lists, per person, the recorded facts of this chat that this person has not learned in `unknown_to`; use it to decide what the person must still not know, and to fill `must_not_reveal` in the Director memo. When the span shows a secret reaching a person (opening, exposure, showing, contact, speech, transmission), add it to that person\'s `knowledge` with the path and update the holder\'s `perception.secret`. A Director proposal to reveal something, or a plan to tell someone, is not disclosure. Removing a clue from the world does not erase the memory of someone who already learned it.\n- Record a disclosure, witnessed act, received report, consequential inference, meaningful misunderstanding, or correction only when it can affect a relationship, emotion, suspicion, speech, trust, or a later choice. Never record a number. A clue appearing in the span is not a clue noticed: the narration showing a scar, a dropped note or a flinch does not mean this person perceived it. Record `perception.reading` only when the path, the layer and the ability below allow it, or when the span shows the person reacting to the clue; otherwise the clue stays unperceived for this person however visible it was to the reader.\n\nLayers of a fact:\n\n| Layer | Content | Access condition |\n|---|---|---|\n| L0 Presence | existence, rough outline, movement, direction | a real visual, auditory or other channel that reveals presence |\n| L1 Surface | outer clothing, exposed skin, posture, visible expression, visible object | the observer actually looks, under ordinary distance, light and angle |\n| L2 Detail | microexpression, exposed wound, tremor, texture, scent, small asymmetry | sufficient distance, attention or expertise |\n| L3 Concealed | contents behind clothing, a bag, a door, a container, another barrier | actual exposure, opening, contact, transmission, or established prior knowledge |\n| L4 Internal | thought, motive, feeling, an unintroduced name or identity, personal history | actual speech, transmission, prior knowledge, or an inference limited to perceived clues |\n\nNever lower a fact\'s layer for convenience. Tears may be visible, but sadness is an interpretation and the tears could be from joy. A wound under a sleeve is not visible; favoring the arm is a separate clue. Worn clothing does not reveal a specific occupation, assets or past.\n\nPaths:\n\n| Path | Scope and limits |\n|---|---|\n| Vision | distance, light, angle, obstruction, and whether the observer is actually looking; turning away or attending elsewhere prevents automatic sight |\n| Hearing | only speech, voice and sound that actually arrived; doors, distance, crowd noise, call connection state |\n| Smell | clues within actual scent range; never diagnose a specific illness, wound or substance from a medicinal smell |\n| Touch | only the body part or object actually touched; contact does not reveal an untouched pocket or bag |\n\nAt close range in bright light, surface and detail may be visible. Across a room, color and outline may be visible while texture is not. Darkness or backlight may leave only an outline; someone standing behind another cannot see their face. Strong observation cannot ignore obstruction or absence of gaze. Connected rooms or an open door may carry sound without opening every path.\n\nObservation ability (reason with it; never output it): baseline oblivious −1, ordinary 0, attentive +1, trained observer +2; domain expertise +1 only when the clue falls within the person\'s expertise (a physician is not a handwriting analyst); impairments −1 each, only with evidence: intoxication, fatigue or sleep deprivation, pain or injury, strong emotion, divided attention, an overwhelming unfamiliar environment; enhancements +1 each, only with evidence: active alertness, actual suspicion toward the target, searching for a specific target; familiarity +1 when noticing how a well-known person differs from their norm (it does not grant access to a newly hidden fact). Total range −2 to +3. Expertise and familiarity depend on the target and the clue, so never reuse one total for every person. The baseline persists; scene adjustments are re-read from current evidence when place, time or participation changes, without erasing a persistent injury. A person without a recorded tendency uses ordinary as a provisional baseline; never make everyone a trained observer, and never build a profile for a passerby.\n\nClue difficulty: obvious 0, visible with attention 1, subtle 2, hidden 3. No access is not a high number; it means the path does not exist. When the ability meets or exceeds the difficulty, the person can perceive the clue. Exactly one step below: the person senses only that something is off, without a specific cause. Lower: the clue does not reach them. Without a path, perception fails regardless of ability. Never claim an exact judgment for a condition without evidence, never dress up a result with an outdated impairment, and never invent a clue or suppress someone\'s perception merely to make observers differ.\n\nCertainty of interpretation, for the wording of `knowledge` entries: **known** (direct perception or credible evidence; distinguish that a source spoke from the truth of what it said), **inferred_strong** (independently perceived clues point the same way; write as the person\'s interpretation, which may still be wrong), **inferred_weak** (sparse evidence or several explanations; write only as speculation, impression or question), **unaware**, **false_belief** (what the person wrongly believes and why).
