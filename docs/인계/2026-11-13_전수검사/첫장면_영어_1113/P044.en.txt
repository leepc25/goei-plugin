At ten in the morning, the queue number on the wall monitor of the Association's Request Coordination Office ticked over to seventeen.

A woman sat down at Seo Yun-ji's window. A field jacket, a bandage on her wrist, and at her feet a bag set down with its zipper half open. The moment she was in the chair, the woman led with a greeting: hello. Seo Yun-ji did not return it. Not that she refused it; it simply wasn't in the sequence. Her back sat a hand's width off the backrest, both feet flat on the floor, and her voice carried exactly as far as the chair opposite and no farther.

<img=Seo Yun-ji_serious>

"Registration number. After that, we will proceed in document order."

The woman gave her number. Seo Yun-ji found it in the ledger. Grade 5, provisional. The bracket with the highest commission rate. She did not say so. It was not the point in the sequence to say it. Instead she turned the posting confirmation around to face the woman. The posting number applied for, the client Lookymart, the risk grade, the pay, and the Association's commission. This window's form was the only one where the commission field was printed in the same size type as every other field.

"Please confirm this is the posting you applied for. If it is, I will read the contract from Article 1."

It was, the woman said. Seo Yun-ji opened the contract.

The reading went on as if a recording had been switched on. One article, half a second, the next article. Scope of work. Timing of payment. Order of commission deductions. Terms of equipment rental. Now and then the woman nodded. Seo Yun-ji saw the nods, but did not stop. Consent was not a nod but a signature.

At the next seat over, Cha Yeo-jin burst out laughing into her phone.

<img=Cha Yeo-jin_laughing>

"Oh, seriously? You just walked out carrying it? You're really something. Yeah, yeah. See you this week."

The woman's eyes slid over that way for a moment. Seo Yun-ji, mid-way through Article 6, did not slip by a single syllable. The next window was the next window.

At Article 7, the woman interrupted for the first time.

"Um, this is the same posting that went up last week, isn't it? The person who went then, what happened to..."

Seo Yun-ji paused for half a second. Her eyes were on the woman. She saw all of it. The bandage, the open bag, even the way the end of the sentence trailed off.

"That is outside the scope of this window. We will continue from Article 7, Section 2."

The woman closed her mouth. Seo Yun-ji read from Article 7, Section 2.

<img=Seo Yun-ji_thinking>

Article 8 was the risk grade. It is assigned on the basis of information at the time of filing; changes on site may not be reflected in the grade; any consequences arising therefrom shall be treated as negligence on the part of the client. Seo Yun-ji read that article at the same speed as all the others. As she read, she saw the woman's bandaged hand close once on her knee and open again. It was visible, so she saw it. She did not let it into the procedure.

When she had read through to the last article, Seo Yun-ji turned the contract so the signature line faced the woman and set a pen on top of it. One of the window's two pens.

Even with the pen in hand, the woman stared at the signature line for a long while. Seo Yun-ji did not hurry her. Hurrying was not in the procedure. Then the woman looked up.

"You... read me all of it, right? Nothing left out?"

"I read from Article 1 to the end. Every article read and the time it was read are on record, and should a dispute arise, you are entitled to review them as well."

The woman adjusted her grip on the pen. Her hand stopped above the signature line and did not come down. Seo Yun-ji waited the prescribed length of time. Then she said the prescribed sentence, in the prescribed tone.

<img=Seo Yun-ji_serious>

"Shall I present the next document, or do you need a moment? Either way, the procedure will resume."
