Evening came early to a neighborhood where redevelopment had stalled. Once the sun dropped between the half-demolished shopping buildings, it was the streetlights' turn to come on, but half the streetlights in this alley were out. The shutters had rusted and sagged shut, and weeds pushing up through the cracks in the paving blocks had grown knee-high. Over everything lay the particular quiet of a neighborhood where no one lived, the kind in which sound carried a long way.

Avensha walked the alley as if out for a stroll. In fact, it was a stroll. Her dress shoes picked their way across the broken paving blocks, and the hem of her cape brushed the tips of the weeds. It was a pleasant evening. That morning someone had posted that a new subway entrance had appeared in this neighborhood, and the first comment, as always, said not to go in. Avensha had read the post over black tea. There was one thing that could be obtained only in that station's shopping concourse. It was an ingredient the mages under her wouldn't recognize even if she told them its name, and she had always obtained ingredients like that the same way. By going in herself, where others said not to go.

White light seeped out from the end of the alley.

It was a light that didn't belong in the run-down neighborhood. Turning the corner, she saw what it was. A subway entrance finished in pure white tile. Stairs without a speck of dust. A sign of three Chinese characters, set perfectly straight. The shop right next to it had its shutter half collapsed, yet this entrance alone was as clean as if the ribbon had been cut on it that very morning. Avensha's eyes sparkled. She was rather fond of clean things.

<img=Avensha_excited>

There were three people in front of the entrance.

A single line of tape was strung between two yellow tripods, and in front of it the three stood, each in a different posture. One was squatting, rummaging through a bag. One was taking photos of the entrance with a phone, checking the screen after every shot and clicking their tongue. The man who looked oldest of the three noticed Avensha first. He wore a hiking jacket over a worn stab vest, and an Association license was clipped to the chest of the jacket. It wasn't worth reading as far as the grade field.

"Excuse me, this area's restricted right now. It's dangerous, so please turn back."

There was no hostility in his voice. It was the tone of someone who said this several times a day. Avensha stopped walking, tilted her head slightly, and looked at him. Then she gave him a bright smile.

<img=Avensha_happy>

"Oh my, how kind of you. Imagine hearing something like that in a neighborhood like this."

The words themselves were a greeting and her voice was sweet, yet the man, on receiving that greeting, couldn't quite get his expression in order. It was the face of someone who couldn't gauge what lay behind a smiling face.

The younger one who had been squatting stood up. All his gear was brand new. The flashlight, the gloves, the radio at his waist. He looked Avensha up and down and snorted.

"What's this, cosplay? Lady, this isn't some event venue. Go on, over that way."

The man grabbed the younger one's shoulder. It was a hand that said not to run your mouth carelessly at strangers on site.

"Hey, watch how you talk. You going to take responsibility if a complaint comes in?"

"Complaint, what complaint. She's just some weird lady."

The younger one shrugged off the hand and stepped out in front of the tape. He blocked the stairs with the arm holding the flashlight. It put him at an angle looking down at Avensha. He was tall.

Avensha looked at that arm. She looked at the face above the arm. Then, with the same smiling face, she flicked one finger.

<img=Avensha_smug>

The younger one made no sound. The flashlight hit the tile first, and the hand that had been holding it crumbled away into fine sand, starting from the wrist. The arm, the shoulder, the open mouth collapsed one after another. The stab vest that had been brand new slumped down, empty inside, and on top of it the sand formed a small hill.
