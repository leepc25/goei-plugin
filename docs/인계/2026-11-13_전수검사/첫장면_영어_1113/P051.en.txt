The neighborhood was empty. Gas leak. A notice saying so stood at the mouth of every alley, with yellow tape strung behind it. The streetlights were on, but not a single house had a lit window. On top of the parked cars, dead flies had piled up instead of fallen leaves. And at the end of the alley, only the sky over where the cathedral stood was red. Even though the hour of sunset had passed three hours before.

Saladin was walking up that alley. Black suit, white turban. A scimitar at his waist, a rifle on his back, a bandolier and explosive pouches across his chest. His pace was as slow as a man out for a stroll, and his footsteps rang loud through the empty neighborhood. It was the walk of someone with no intention of hiding. Nor any need to.

No one had told him, and no one had asked him. Since the night three days ago, he had been able to see, from the mosque courtyard, the sky in that direction glowing red. The first night he had thought it was sunset; on the second, seeing the same spot red at the same hour, he knew it was not. On the third night, Saladin took the scimitar down from the wall. Whatever it was that wore a sky like that over its head, he would know once he went. He would go and burn it.

A patrol car was parked in front of the tape. Two officers. One was dozing inside the car; the other was smoking outside when he saw a silhouette coming up the alley. The cigarette fell from his hand. Beard, turban, a gun barrel slung over the shoulder. A face he had seen on the news. More precisely, a face he thought he had seen on the news.

"S-sir! Stop right there! Hands where I can see them!"

The officer's hand went to his hip. Saladin did not break stride. He came up to the tape and looked down at the officer. The officer's head came to about Saladin's chest.

Saladin spoke. His voice was booming, old-fashioned, and courteous.

"Beyond this point is no place for people to enter. Turn your car around, the two of you, and wait out by the main road. Whatever sounds you hear in this neighborhood tonight, do not come up. Take it for thunder."

Before the officer could answer, Saladin lifted the tape with one hand and passed beneath it. Behind him came radio static and the sound of car doors opening and closing. He did not look back.

The closer he came to the wall, the heavier the air grew. The buzz of fly wings never stopped beside his ears, and the grass underfoot was all dead and yellowed. From among the dead grass, two red dots looked up at Saladin. A black rat. It did not run like other rats. Only when Saladin took another step did it turn, and it was not fleeing but running toward the cathedral. It was going to tell. A black snake coiled around a dead branch on top of the wall dropped from the branch with a thud as Saladin passed. Where it fell, it did not so much as twitch.

Something was seeping out of Saladin's body. Not something visible. Only, the flies in the places he passed dropped to the ground, and from the darkness inside the wall came the sense of large things all drawing back at once. The flock of crows circling above the cathedral scattered. The red sky stayed as it was.

He crossed the yard and stood before the main door. On the outside, it was an ordinary wooden door. A cross-shaped handle, nail marks, old varnish. A tuft of black hair had grown on the handle.

Saladin laid his palm on the door.

<img=Saladin_contemptuous>

The door was warm. The warmth of a human body. Beneath his palm, the door slowly swelled and then subsided. It was breathing. Saladin did not take his hand away. His other hand went to the hilt of the scimitar at his waist.

"The wicked god shall burn here!"
