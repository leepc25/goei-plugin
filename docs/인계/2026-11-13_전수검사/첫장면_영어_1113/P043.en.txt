Lunch had only just ended in the Request Coordination Office.

Beneath the sign of a disaster consulting firm in Yeongdeungpo, at the desk beside the reception window, Cha Yeo-jin was paging through the list of newly arrived requests. This hour of the afternoon was the best part of her day. Replies to the check-in texts she had sent out that morning were trickling in one by one, and the request list had just gone up, untouched by anyone. On the wall monitor, unassigned requests scrolled past, the pay in bold type and the commission in small type beneath it. It was the hour for matching up pairs: who to show what.

<img=Cha Yeo-jin_thinking>

One in the middle of the list caught her eye.

The client was the family of a missing person. With no money for a deposit, the younger brother had been making the rounds of the flophouse alleys, and the last anyone heard from him was a text: "Found a cheap room, Saetbyeol Gosiwon (샛별 고시원), move in right away." After that, nothing. The request: confirm his whereabouts and, if possible, bring him back. The pay was large for money a family like that could put up, and the risk rating sat in the second box from the top. In the remarks field, in the Association's handwriting, one line had been added. Grade 3 assigned last week did not return; processed as client-side fault.

Saetbyeol Gosiwon. She had never heard the name, and she had no need to know it. What mattered to Cha Yeo-jin was that one line in the remarks field. If a Grade 3 went in and didn't come out, the next Grade 3 would read that line and pass. So requests like this one were always left over, and on a leftover request she could pile on a bigger commission and the family wouldn't say a word. They didn't know what the Association's commission schedule looked like.

Cha Yeo-jin took a leather notebook from the inside pocket of her jacket. Her finger stopped on a name that had come to the window for the second time yesterday. Someone who had come in yesterday wearing the same jumper as on the first visit. The corner of a pawnshop receipt poking out of a pocket; "I'll do it" out before half the commission explanation was through. Grade 5, provisional. And when they parted yesterday, they had said it themselves: they were short on the room deposit, so for now they were sleeping at a jjimjilbang.

<img=Cha Yeo-jin_smile>

The request said move in right away. Cha Yeo-jin crossed out the number beside that name and wrote one bigger. Holding out a request with a room attached to someone who badly needed a room: there would be no better match than that this month.

She pulled up a draft assignment form and made a summary for the window. She copied the request details over as they were. The remarks field was not one of the fields included in a window summary. That was the rule. If you knew the rules by heart, you also knew which field was visible to whom, and how far. She left the risk-rating field alone. She had something else ready to say to anyone who asked.

"Risk rating? That's inflated for the paperwork. It'll be done before lunch, honestly."

The phone in her trouser pocket rang. A different ringtone from the one on the desk. Cha Yeo-jin glanced once at the glass door of the window, swung her chair half around, and answered with her back turned.

<img=Mun Hye-ran_laughing>

"Hey, hey, Coordinator Cha! Eaten yet? Doesn't matter, listen. We're short a body on our end, urgent. That kid in the jumper you mentioned last week, still sitting idle?"

Cha Yeo-jin laughed. Half a beat late.

"Ah, that one I'm using today. But something similar should come in next week, so I'll pass that one your way."

On the other end of the line, something went *tok*, once. Cha Yeo-jin knew it was the sound of a gold watch being tapped. That she'd pull that even on her was funny, and she laughed again.

"Next week? Hahaha! My customers don't go hungry till next week, friend. Fine, then let's do it this way. You use that one. But if they come back, the job after that is ours. Finder's fee as usual. If they don't come back, well, that's as usual too."

"Fine. As usual."

Neither of them bothered to point out the condition, if they come back.

She hung up and turned the chair back around.

At the next desk, Seo Yun-ji was taking a call on her own window line. Her back did not touch the backrest, and her voice was pitched to carry only as far as the chair across from her.

<img=Seo Yun-ji_serious>

"Please give me your registration number first. Then we will proceed in the order of the documents."

Half a second's pause, then the next question in the same tone. It sounded like a recording being played. There was a saying: go to that window and you'd cry from boredom, come to this one and you'd cry for a different reason. Cha Yeo-jin laughed every time she heard it. Seo Yun-ji was the only one who didn't know that having the boring window right next door made hers look all the kinder. She did not turn her chair that way.

She picked up the phone on the desk. The one that was on the registry. She found yesterday's name and wrote a text. It was the wording she always used. She always made sure to put the name at the very front.

"That commission worry you mentioned yesterday, something came in that might ease it a little. Honestly, I don't show this request to just anyone. The moment I saw your file, you were the first one I thought of. It might sort out your lodging problem too. If you've got time today, come by the window."

Call them by name on the second visit, and they will always come a third time.

She hit send and laid the phone face down.

<img=Cha Yeo-jin_smirk>

The reply did not take long. "Coming now." One line, and no question mark. Cha Yeo-jin drew the empty chair in front of the window toward her with her fingertips and straightened its angle, then printed the summary. A single sheet with the remarks field left out came warm from the printer. On it, the words "Saetbyeol Gosiwon" were stamped just below the bold type of the pay.
