Eleven o'clock, Tuesday night. Only half the studio lights were on.

Brownie stood before the mirror backstage. Calling it a mirror was generous: it was his own reflection in a switched-off screen, but it was enough to fix the knot of his necktie. He brushed the lapels of his brown three-piece twice. He gave each glove a single tug. For a broadcaster, wardrobe is everything.

A staffer whose face was smeared into a blur held up a cue card. Seven contestants. All arrived. Brownie put a smiling face up on his screen.

<img=Brownie_happy>

**[Seven! A full house. Good, good. I've got a feeling about tonight.]**

He walked out onto the stage. Seven people stood at seven podiums arranged in a semicircle. They all had the faces of people who had only just arrived. They shaded their brows against the glare, looked down at the gaudy podiums under their feet, looked at the person beside them, looked back at the lights. Their hands still bore pen marks. Hands that had come straight from writing names on postcards.

Brownie made the rounds from the first podium on, holding out a gloved hand. He didn't skip a single one.

**[Welcome, welcome! Oh, your hand's cold. Nervous, huh. That's fine, nerves are the seasoning of a good broadcast. Just call me Brownie. We're friends here, so let's drop the cringey honorifics!]**

The contestant at the fourth podium pulled their hand back to avoid the handshake. Brownie waited for that hand. The smiling face on his screen didn't change in the slightest. In the end, the contestant held out their hand. The glove closed around it.

**[That's it. Just one handshake. We hold hands, and we go together.]**

The staffer folded down fingers: three, two, one. Every light came on. The band burst into play.

<img=Brownie_excited>

**[Good evening, viewers at home! The joy of Tuesday, the heat of Tuesday! You are now watching Brownie's Tuesday Quiz Show!]**

Cheers poured down from the audience seats. Beyond the lights their shapes were mush, but the sound filled the room. Brownie spread his arms and took that sound in for three beats. Then he lowered his hands and cut it off. The band cut off with him.

**[Tonight's seven new faces! Give them a hand! Louder! That's it!]**

*Ppa-bam-bam-ba-bam!* With the sting, a spotlight swept the podiums one by one. Pajamas, work clothes, a school uniform, a suit, an apron, a tracksuit, and, at the fourth podium, a leather jacket. Brownie looked at the leather jacket one more time. It smelled of the Otherworld. He didn't go so far as to ask which organization they belonged to. Here, everyone was a contestant.

**[All right, the rules are simple! The question goes up, you hit the buzzer within a hundred seconds, and you give the right answer! Get it right and it's applause and confetti. Get it wrong and... hm. The penalty changes every week! I haven't even opened this week's penalty envelope myself. Exciting, isn't it?]**

The audience laughed. The contestants did not. Brownie always liked that contrast. Good television comes from a difference in temperature.

<img=Brownie_laughing>

**[Oh, and one more thing. Did you know we haven't had a single wrong answer in the last few dozen weeks? Amazing, right? Simply amazing. Will the streak go on tonight, or will it be broken this very evening!]**

*Ppa-bam!* The band hit a short sting. Brownie returned to center stage. The smiling face vanished from his screen and words appeared in its place. All seven contestants pressed a hand to their foreheads at the same moment. They do it every time. It's a cute sight.

**[Question number one! Which organ in the human body is the first to begin rotting?]**

A clock appeared in the corner of the screen. 100. 99. 98.

**[One hundred seconds! Band, drum roll!]**
