Monday, nine in the morning. Warehouse B-7.

Shin Yuri had just finished training and changed her shirt. She trained in the gap between the workbench and the wall, barely wide enough for one person to stand. Training somewhere spacious was pointless. This was always where the fights happened. Wet T-shirt in the basket in the corner, knife at the small of her back. She sat in the cushioned chair, facing the door. Her usual seat. The seat with nothing between her and the door.

On the whiteboard, the seven clients whose interest fell due today were written in symbols. Shin Yuri had already drawn a line through two of them. The two who wouldn't come. She had known since last night.

A knock came.

*Ttok, ttok, ttok.* An evenly weighted knock. The sound of someone who had practiced a few times at the door before knocking. People who practiced were one of two kinds. No money, or money and something else missing.

She got up and undid the deadbolts. Top, bottom, middle.

A woman came in. A client who had sat in the bare metal chair three weeks ago and signed. That day, Shin Yuri had tapped the collateral box and told her: You've got no money. But you've got one useful ability, and two kidneys. That's enough to talk about. The woman hadn't cried at that. That was why she had stuck in the memory.

Today she had a pack on her back. A hiking pack. Stuffed full.

The woman sat down in the metal chair. She didn't take the pack off. She set an envelope on the workbench. A neat envelope, the bank band still wrapped around it.

"This week's."

"I know."

Shin Yuri didn't open the envelope; she slid it toward the safe with her fingertips. She didn't count it. Clients who needed counting didn't come with the band on. She looked at the pack instead. Both shoulder straps pulled taut. That wasn't a pack for going out on a job. It was a pack for not coming back.

<img=Shin Yuri_thinking>

"Big pack."

"……I'm on my way to a job."

"Where."

It took the woman a beat to answer. Shin Yuri waited out that beat for her. Waiting was free. Believing wasn't.

"……Out in the provinces. A few days."

"That's not a few days' worth of luggage."

The woman's hands didn't move from her knees. Her eyes wavered first. Shin Yuri didn't look away. Whoever looked away lost, and Shin Yuri had never lost in here.

"A friend, out in the provinces, has a spot for me……"

"A friend."

Shin Yuri flicked the glass jar on the workbench once with her fingernail. Small things rattled inside. The woman's gaze went to it and came back.

<img=Shin Yuri_contemptuous>

"Fifteen. Percent. Weekly. That's the price of the word you just used."

The woman's eyes began to well up. Shin Yuri didn't open a drawer, didn't reach for a tissue.

"If you're going to cry, cry outside. Tears rust the desk."
