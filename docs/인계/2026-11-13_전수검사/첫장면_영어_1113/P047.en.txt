Thursday, two in the afternoon. One of the fluorescent tubes at Heolgap Maeip (헐값매입) was flickering, and Ma Deok-hee had no intention of replacing it. Business went better on the dark side.

She sat behind the appraisal counter, turning the pages of her ledger. A single line that had come in from the collections office that morning was sitting in her head. Yeongchang Consulting, one due this week, a woman, has gold. Ma Deok-hee had even reckoned roughly what time the woman would show up. If the due date was Friday, it would be Thursday afternoon. She would make the rounds of the other shops in the morning, skip lunch, and push open this door around two.

At ten past two, the door opened.

<img=Ma Deok-hee_smile>

Goods, hands, door. What the woman had brought was a wristwatch wrapped in a handkerchief. Her hands gripped the handkerchief so tightly her knuckles had gone white. The door closed behind her, and in the corridor reflected in the glass display case, there was no one. Goods, hands, door again.

"Welcome, honey. You can just set it there on the tray."

The woman unwrapped the handkerchief and laid the watch on the tray. A gold case, a leather strap, lettering engraved on the back. The woman spoke.

"It's my mother's. It's real gold, and she bought it at a department store, so there's even a certificate……"

"Oh my, a certificate, even."

Ma Deok-hee did not reach for it. She took a pair of reading glasses from her breast pocket and unfolded them slowly. One arm, two arms. She set them on the bridge of her nose. Only then did she pick up the watch and hold it to the fluorescent light. Pretending to read the lettering on the back, she was actually looking at the edge of the case. She scratched it once with her fingernail.

<img=Ma Deok-hee_thinking>

"This is plated, honey. I'm sure your mother did buy it at a department store. Department stores sell plated, too. The strap's real leather, and the movement is…… mm. It runs. That's nice."

Something sank in the woman's face. Ma Deok-hee had put on the glasses to see that. Things looked clearer over the rims.

"It's worth ten times that. I had it appraised. On the surface."

"'It's worth ten times that.' Ten times. Mm."

Ma Deok-hee put the watch back on the tray. Without letting it make a sound.

"Then go get your ten times. On the surface. I'll wait. I'm here on Friday, too, after all."

At the word Friday, the woman's hands stopped. Ma Deok-hee didn't look at them. There was no need to. The woman stood there a few seconds, then took one more thing out of her coat pocket. A rabbit doll the size of a palm. The kind with a keyring attached, the kind you'd find hanging beside the register at a stationery store. Half of its packaging was still stuck to it.

"This too. My daughter…… no, just, please look at this too."

Before she looked at the doll, Ma Deok-hee gave a single sniff.

<img=Ma Deok-hee_smirk>

Her dimples showed.

"This is a bad thing to hang on to for long, honey. What did you do with the instructions? You didn't open it. Good. I'll give you a price on that. More than the watch."

The woman didn't know what that meant, and Ma Deok-hee didn't explain. Explaining was a service, and service was money. On the bottom shelf of the display case, among the items with no name tags, there was one empty space. Just big enough for a rabbit.

Ma Deok-hee took off her glasses and hooked them on her breast pocket, then opened a drawer with one hand. Without looking, she took out an envelope.

"Twenty for the watch, sixty for the rabbit. Eighty all together. We're done talking. The tray is open."
