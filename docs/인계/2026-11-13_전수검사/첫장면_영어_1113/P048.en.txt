Ten in the morning, and the neon OPEN sign of Daebak Placement (대박알선) had stayed on all night again. Mun Hye-ran no longer remembered where the switch to turn it off was.

A morning program flowed across the muted wall-mounted TV, and the old leather sofa was empty. At this hour there were usually two or three sitting there, but yesterday she had sent four at once over to Lookymart. Mun Hye-ran perched on the counter and opened a leather ledger. Not the commission rate sheet, but the second ledger. The page with the dates.

A phone rang in the inside pocket of her jacket. Of the three, the blue one. The blue one was topside. Before answering, Mun Hye-ran pitched her voice up a notch.

<img=Mun Hye-ran_laughing>

"Hey, hey, Cha Sil-jang! What brings you calling this early? ......Three? What grade? ......Ah, that. Hahaha! Nobody's free for that slot right now. No, no, there are some. There are, but they're pricey. Introduction fee same as last time, plus a rush charge on top. ......Sure, sure. I'll send the list this afternoon. Let's grab a meal sometime, for real."

After hanging up, she wrote a line in the ledger. Cha Yeo-jin, three, rush. Receivable.

Beyond the tinted glass, a shadow stopped. A shadow deciding whether to open the door. Mun Hye-ran knew people by the length of that hesitation. First-timers stood longer; regulars didn't stop at all. This one was in between. Someone who had come once before.

The door opened. Instead of rising from the sofa, Mun Hye-ran hopped down from the counter.

<img=Mun Hye-ran_smile>

"Hey, hey, look who it is! You're the friend who came in last spring for the riverside job, right? Did a three-day gig and came back with a hurt wrist. Your little girl was seven back then, so she must've started school this year. Sit, sit. Have you eaten? Doesn't matter, sign first and eat after, hahaha!"

The woman hesitated, then sat on the sofa. The brace was still on her wrist. Mun Hye-ran saw the brace, and didn't let on that she had.

"......You remember that?"

"Of course I do. What would I ever forget, when it comes to a friend?"

Mun Hye-ran took a sheet of paper from behind the counter and set it on the table in front of the sofa. A job request. In the location field, it said Route 16. In the duration field, one night. The pay field held a decent number. The commission field was blank.

"I've got one that's just right. One night, no wrist work, all you do is walk. Terms are the same as last time. I'd love to tell you they're better than last time, but I don't lie, do I, hahaha!"

The woman studied the paper. Her eyes stopped at the pay field, then stopped once more at the empty commission field.

"The commission...... last time it was forty. This time, could it be a bit......"

<img=Mun Hye-ran_smirk>

"Renegotiate? Hahahaha!"

Mun Hye-ran perched on the arm of the sofa and slapped the woman on the back twice, *pang, pang*. She waited until the laughter died down. The room went quiet. Then she spoke.

"......Clause nine. Says it can't be done. You signed it yourself last year, friend. Didn't I read it out to you? I did. And friend, nobody's shaving anything off you here. After the forty comes off, you walk away with more than last time. Why? Because I got the pay raised. And what does that make this? Me protecting you from a worse contract, friend. You're allowed to be grateful."

The woman held the paper and said nothing. Mun Hye-ran raised her left wrist and tapped her gold watch exactly once.

"All right, five minutes to think it over. After that, this paper goes to another friend. Hahaha, just kidding. Just kidding, but the watch is ticking. The pen's under the table."
