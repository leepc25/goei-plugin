Eleven at night. Above the operating room door at Songil Clinic, the red light was on.

Inside, the Director was operating. Yun Ji-a sat at the reception window folding gauze. When her hands were empty, she folded gauze. The folded gauze had already filled two drawers and spilled over, so lately she had started on a third. She tugged once at the black hair tie on her left wrist and let it snap back, then unzipped the pouch at her waist and counted the candies. Five. A lot had gone out today.

One person sat on the plastic chairs in the waiting room. Someone who had come in with the patient now in surgery, she'd said. A sheath showed through the inside of her leather jacket, and her hands, resting on her knees, kept lacing and unlacing their fingers without pause. And she kept looking at the back wall. Toward the spot beside the double-locked medicine cabinet, toward the refrigerator big enough to fit a person.

Yun Ji-a poured a cup of water and came out from behind the window.

"Here, drink this. The Director's handling things in there, so the only thing anyone can do out here is drink water."

The woman took the cup. Her eyes were still on the refrigerator. Yun Ji-a stepped into the path of that gaze and blocked it, casually. She took a candy from her pouch and held it out.

"You don't need to look over there. It's a specimen storage unit, according to the Director. Unwrap one of these. It's grape today. People make bets guessing the flavor and stuff."

The woman took the candy. There was the sound of the wrapper coming off, and the lacing hands went still. Yun Ji-a made sure of that and went back to the window. Reading a waiting room wasn't something she'd learned in school. She'd learned it here.

In the reception desk drawer, her phone vibrated. A friend from aboveground. What are you up to, working? Yun Ji-a thumbed out a reply.

<img=Yun Ji-a_thinking>

Yeah, night shift. Work was kind of weird today. Weird how... just weird. Anyway, what about you? What are you doing this weekend?

Send. The friend didn't ask for the address, and she had no intention of giving it. She put the phone back in the drawer. Deeper in the drawer were three nursing textbooks. Books underlined through the first semester of third year. Applications for next semester opened next month. She could do it then. Probably.

From inside the operating room came the sound of metal being set down on a tray. One, two. Yun Ji-a counted the progress by sound. Suturing now. Thirty minutes to go.

The woman in the waiting room spoke up.

"Um... the kid in there, are they going to be okay?"

<img=Yun Ji-a_happy>

Yun Ji-a answered without stopping her hands from folding gauze.

"That's not something the Director sells, so I can't say it for her. But there are things you can tell by sound, and right now she's started suturing. Suturing's what the Director is fastest at. Want some more water?"
