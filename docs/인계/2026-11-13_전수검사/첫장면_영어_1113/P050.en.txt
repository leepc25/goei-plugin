A transfer station cools quickly once the last train has gone. The glow of the vending machine at the end of the platform, the swish of a cleaner's mop, and the shop shutters going dark one by one along the passage. Eleven-forty. Blaret stood with her back against the pillar in the middle of the platform. Still in her nun's habit. Through the side slit, the silver-loaded pistol in her garter holster showed, then hid, then showed again, and the silver cross that hung from her neck she had tucked inside her coat. A cleaner passing by made the sign of the cross and moved on. Blaret gave a little wave.

Three days now at this pillar. She had traced the smell that had been carried into the shop back upstream, and it led to this station, and inside this station, to this pillar. The smell pooled here. Not the trace of something that had fled. The trace of something that had vanished. Telling those two apart was the first thing Blaret had learned in this line of work, and the place where a vanished trace pooled was usually an entrance. The problem was the grain of the smell. A vampire, no question, but for a vampire it ran gamy rather than bloody. There was the smell of an animal pen mixed into it.

"Um, Sister. The last train's gone."

A station attendant. Blaret turned her head and smiled at him.

"I know. I'm waiting for someone."

The attendant started to say something more, then looked toward the stairs and shut his mouth.

The footsteps coming down the stairs were one set, but they echoed like three. A black suit, a white turban, the barrel of the rifle slung on the back flashing under the ceiling lights. The scimitar at the waist pushed the coat's hem aside with every step. The attendant raised his walkie-talkie, then, seeing the man step down onto the platform and look straight at Blaret, lowered it again. And went up the stairs. Quickly.

It was Saladin.

Blaret did not take her back off the pillar. She only raised a hand and waved. It was the voice she used for customers at the shop.

"Oh my, how nice to see you! What brings you all the way down here from the mosque at this hour?"

Saladin's hand was already on the hilt of his scimitar.

"What is a heathen doing here at this hour?"

"Hunting. Same as you, isn't it? There's no way you caught this smell from up on the mosque hill, so somebody must have gone all the way up to the mosque to tattle."

Saladin's jaw tightened once. Apparently she was right.

<img=Saladin_angry>

"Those who sought out your shop and those who came up to the mosque named the same station. That is enough. If there is something beneath here that devours people, and it is of the blood-drinking kind, then it is my business as well."

Blaret heard him out to the end, then laughed.

"Vampires are our specialty. It's the subject the Papal Court has been hanging onto for all of history. You just keep a good watch on your mosque."

And then she added what she always said.

"How about it? Give up that Islam business and convert over to our side?"

Saladin's fingers closed on the hilt and opened again. Blaret's hand had gone inside the side slit. Each was watching the other's hand.

"Hold your tongue, Sister. I did not come to fight you tonight."

"Neither did I."

Blaret said it, but she did not take her hand out.

<img=Blaret_smirk>

That was when the light of the vending machine at the end of the platform went out.

It was not a malfunction. Not only the light had gone, but the hum the vending machine made had vanished with it. The swish of the cleaner's mop was gone too, at some point. And the radio that had been trickling out of the station office at the top of the stairs. When Blaret drew a breath, it came out as fog. Breath had no business fogging on an underground platform.

<img=Saladin_serious>

Saladin took his hand off the hilt. Instead he swung the rifle from his back around to his front. Blaret, leaving her hand where it was inside the side slit, turned toward where the vending machine had gone dark.
