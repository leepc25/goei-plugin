It was a transfer corridor, past midnight. The underground arcade, shutters half down, was dark, and only the smell of floor polish lingered. Secriem walked the corridor with her high heels clicking. She made the sound on purpose. Seeing whether anyone at this hour heard it and turned around was part of the job.

<img=Secriem_bored>

She had abandoned the station she'd been using until last month. That was after a single sheet of paper went up on the restroom door: "If the door is locked, please contact the station office." Who put it there, she didn't know. After that paper, fewer hands knocked on the door, and Secriem took it as an insult. A spot that had worked for decades, ruined by one sheet of paper.

So tonight she had come out to scout a new spot.

Walking the corridor to its end, she looked up at the ceiling. One camera toward the ticket gates and one on the stair landing; the corner at the end of the corridor showed up on neither. Tucked inside that corner was a staff door. It was marked STORAGE. When Secriem gripped the handle, it was locked.

"Nice. It has to be a locked door."

<img=Secriem_smile>

Call for help from behind an open door and no one believes you. There were ways to get inside without opening a door. Secriem's body scattered, thinning as if sucked through the gap in the door, and gathered again on the other side. A few bats slipped through the gap late and returned beneath the hem of her skirt. The storage room was cramped, stacked with mops and detergent drums. From the inside, the door opened with a turn of the handle. It was locked only from the outside. That was good, too. Calling for someone to open it, from behind a door you could open with your own hand and walk out of: that was the whole game.

Secriem sat on a detergent drum and crossed her legs. And she waited.

Waiting was what Secriem did best. Catching from one spot for ten years paid better than catching one a night. Whether this station became that kind of spot would be decided by the first customer. A stupid one, she would drink and discard; a useful one, she would bite and keep at her side. That was how a family grew, and when the family grew, a house of her own would stand on this land. A house of her own, under no one.

<img=Secriem_annoyed>

Inside the storage room, she counted the sounds outside one by one. The ventilation fan turning, a shutter somewhere far off dropping another notch, the noise the fluorescent light over the stair landing made as it flickered. No sound of a mop. That meant no one cleaned this corridor at this hour, and it also meant no one had to open this door every day. That had been the problem with the old station. When the person who opens the door every day disappears, the station gets noisy.

<img=Secriem_smirk>

Secriem steadied her voice. The same tone for decades now. Thin, a little shaky, the kind of voice that made the listener believe they were the stronger one. From inside the door, she scratched it twice with her fingernails.

"Hello? I think I'm locked in here. Could you open this for me?"
